### 6.a — Bozza risposta a Jeremy Schleicher (in inglese)

**Chi è Jeremy**: Senior Oracle DBA (CISA + CRISC + Security+), profilo LinkedIn `linkedin.com/in/jeremyschleicher`, connessione 2nd degree verified. **Suo commento** (5h prima dell'analisi): *"How did you deal with tablespace encryption and any wallets?"*

**Bozza commento** (6 righe, tono peer-to-peer):

> Great catch Jeremy — I skipped TDE in the article to keep the focus on the transport mechanics, but you're right that it's the piece that decides whether the window opens or not on any regulated workload.
>
> Short version of the path we used: `ADMINISTER KEY MANAGEMENT EXPORT ENCRYPTION KEYS` on the 12.2 source (scoped by tablespace key_id), secure transfer of the `.exp`, then `IMPORT` inside the target PDB — with `WALLET_ROOT` set at CDB level and `TDE_CONFIGURATION` on the PDB, so the sqlnet.ora legacy path stays out of the picture on 21c.
>
> The trap I'd flag for anyone doing this the first time: the keystore must be open and the keys imported **before** you attempt to bring the encrypted tablespaces online — otherwise ORA-28365 lands exactly when the window is closing.
>
> Would make a good follow-up post on its own — happy to trade notes if you've hit corner cases with auto-login wallets across the CDB/PDB boundary.

**Nota di trasparenza**: se nel caso reale raccontato nell'articolo NON c'era TDE, riformulare in condizionale (*"the path I'd follow (and have followed on similar TDE workloads)…"* invece di *"the path we used"*) — più onesto e comunque credibile. Ivan decide in base al ricordo del progetto reale.
